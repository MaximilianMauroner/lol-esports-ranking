# One connected League of Legends intelligence platform

The product helps people make decisions about League of Legends through connected evidence. A player can review a purchase, a coach can prepare for an opponent, and a scout can compare candidates. Each should be able to inspect the history, assumptions, and source records behind an assessment, save a decision, and review later evidence.

The product has four connected categories: esports intelligence and scouting, builds and gameplay optimization, personal performance analytics, and an installed Windows and macOS companion. All four belong to the product's purpose. Their inclusion is a target, not a claim that they are implemented or must ship together. The [product specification](product-specification.md) defines their requirements, acceptance conditions, limits, and open decisions.

## People and decisions

Everyday and ranked players need to understand repeated patterns in their games and choose a useful improvement focus. Competitive players need opponent preparation, champion history, and situational purchase comparisons. Coaches and analysts need to examine team performance, lineups, drafts, and the matches that support an assessment.

Scouts and organizations need to compare candidates within a stated role and competitive context. They need verified identities, uncertainty, and a clear boundary between recorded performance and private reviewer opinion. Esports followers need rankings, trajectories, schedules, results, brackets, and forecasts where the evidence supports them.

These audiences share questions about performance and choices, but their contexts differ. A professional player's ranked account can add champion and practice evidence. It cannot make ranked results equivalent to professional competition. No first commercial audience has been selected.

## Preparation, play, and review

A coach starts with an upcoming opponent. The team profile shows the selected lineup, recent form, champion pools, and draft tendencies. The coach opens the matches behind a pattern and checks the players' prior champion experience. A supported draft comparison uses only information available at that draft state. The coach can attach relevant games, clips, and saved comparisons to a preparation report.

A scout can follow the same evidence into a candidate's professional profile and verified ranked accounts. The account connection preserves the distinction between competition types. A candidate report separates observations, adjusted estimates, reviewer interpretation, and unresolved questions. Private notes remain private when a report links to public evidence.

A player can prepare from a saved champion or build plan on the website, then open the same plan in the companion. The patch, assumptions, version, and permissions travel with it. During play, the companion stays quiet within measured resource limits. Manual preparation and review remain useful when client detection, overlays, and recording are unavailable or declined.

After the game, the player opens a match review once source data is ready. A purchase checkpoint can become a simulation with the observed inventory, level, gold, and known enemy state. The player compares affordable choices for a level-14 fight, then checks how each choice affects later purchases. Missing fields remain visible. A comparison that uses later information is labeled as hindsight.

The player saves the comparison with its assumptions and chooses a measurable improvement focus. Later games use the same metric and comparable context to show change. A coach or authorized scout can reuse that evidence in a report without copying a number away from its source. Observed improvement does not prove that one purchase, recommendation, or product feature caused it.

## History must change the analysis

History is an input to relevant assessments. Champion experience, recent form, recurring decisions, and competitive context should influence those assessments when suitable evidence exists. Placing a match-history table beside generic champion statistics does not meet this purpose.

Personalization must remain explainable. A player's history may inform a gold trajectory or a supported execution assumption. The player can inspect and override those assumptions. Identical champion, inventory, and combat inputs produce identical mechanical calculations for every account. The product must not invent account-specific damage modifiers or make current habits a permanent limit on improvement.

## Meaning that must survive expansion

A person, a game account, an organization, and a lineup have separate identities. Transfers and role changes must preserve dated history. A game, series, and tournament remain separate outcome units. Names help people find records but do not prove identity or account ownership.

Team strength, individual performance, recent form, draft assessment, metric percentile, and outcome probability answer different questions. Every assessment needs a definition, scope, sample, version, uncertainty where relevant, and a route to its evidence. A missing observation is not zero. Low coverage must not produce an invented neutral grade.

A historical reconstruction differs from a forecast demonstrably published before play. A hypothetical tournament result must not enter actual match history. Popular purchases, outcome associations, and simulated combat results also have different meanings. A simulated damage advantage does not establish a match win-rate increase or a universally best build.

The product must not claim hidden matchmaking rating, a player's true rank, personality, motivation, or unsupported causal explanations. Supported competitions, queues, patches, mechanics, and evidence types must be explicit. Automation must show stale data, provider failures, and unsupported calculations. It must preserve a recoverable valid result when an update fails.

## Trust and access

Saved work joins the categories through shared identities, context, versioned evidence, and permissions. Moving between web and desktop must preserve those meanings. Corrections can create a new analysis while an older report remains pinned to its original evidence.

Public records, authorized account data, private team material, and annotations require distinct access rules. Optional client access, capture, microphone use, and uploads need separate consent. Installing the companion does not authorize live advice or recording. Offline copies have retention and revocation limits that the product must state honestly.

A useful free product is part of the intent. Paid access can add depth, usage, and organizational workflows. Definitions, source limitations, uncertainty, essential privacy controls, and the companion's quit and disable controls must remain available. Prices, quotas, and tier boundaries remain open.

Broader coverage depends on evidence, source rights, feature-specific permissions, validation, and measured costs. Resource efficiency requires whole-process measurements on both advertised desktop platforms. The product should grow through complete connected workflows while preserving these constraints.
