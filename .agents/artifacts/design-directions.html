Five design directions

Five independent directions, each targeting one part of the page. They do not depend on each other, so pick any subset. Every mock is rendered live at both a desktop width and a 390px phone width, on the product's real palette and the new token scale.

B. Mobile card listStop squeezing a desktop table onto a phone. Rankings, mobile only
C. Season timelineTwo rows of scope tabs become one. Scope bar, all views
D. Stat strip, no railGive the table the 340px the rail takes. Rankings layout
E. Compare dockMake the product's best feature visible before you use it. Rankings and Regions

A Score-led board row

Problem. Every cell in the row carries the same weight: rank, crest, name, score, movement, sparkline, record, pick button all sit at roughly one size and one colour. The Power score is what the whole product exists to produce, and it reads as one number among six. Eight columns also means the first thing to break on a narrow screen is the data.

  • Two lines per row instead of eight columns. Line one is identity and score, line two is the supporting evidence.
  • Score at --t-6 weight 800, right-aligned, the only large number in the row.
  • Movement becomes a signed chip next to the score rather than a column with its own sparkline.
  • Nothing needs hiding below 640px, because there are no columns to drop.

B Mobile card list

Problem. Mobile currently renders the desktop table with columns progressively removed. Below 640px the record column disappears, the sparkline disappears, the rank context pill disappears, and type drops to 0.6rem to fit. Below 360px the whole movement column goes. A phone user ends up with less information than the table was built to carry, in the least readable type on the page.

  • Not a table. A list of cards, laid out for the width they actually get.
  • Every field survives: rank, crest, name, league, tier, score, uncertainty, record, movement.
  • Form shown as five outcome pips, which costs almost no width and is faster to read than "27-8".
  • Tap target is the whole card. The compare control is a corner button, not a 44px column stealing width from the name.

C Season timeline

Problem. The scope bar is up to eight controls across two logical groups, and it sits between the page title and the data on every view. It also hides the one thing a season selector should make obvious: where the current split sits in the year, and how far through it we are.

  • The season becomes a single pill. Years are rarely switched, so they do not deserve three permanent tabs.
  • Splits become one continuous track. Position carries the ordering, so each segment needs a label but not a box.
  • The ongoing split shows real progress, which the current "Ongoing" text cannot.
  • "Full year" stops being a tab competing with the splits and becomes the track's own label.
  • On a phone the whole thing collapses to one pill that opens a sheet.

D Stat strip, no rail

Problem. The 320px rail costs the table a fifth of its width on a 1440px screen, which is why the board needs a 660px minimum and starts dropping columns. On a phone the rail is not a rail at all: it becomes three panels stacked below the board that most people never scroll to.

  • Rail contents become four tiles in a strip above the board. The table then goes full width and gains about 340px.
  • Tier density becomes a single proportional bar with counts, which is what it was trying to be. It stays clickable to filter.
  • On a phone the strip is a horizontal snap-scroller, so the same information sits above the board instead of far below it.
  • This is the direction that also fixes the mobile tier legend ordering, without moving the legend into the board.

E Compare dock

Problem. Comparison is the most interesting thing the product does, and nothing on a first visit says it exists. The tray only appears after a pick, and the first thing it shows is a disabled primary button. Then the answer arrives in a full-screen drawer, so you cannot see the board and the comparison at once.

  • A slim dock is always present under the scope bar with two empty slots, so the feature announces itself.
  • The moment two teams are in, the headline answer appears inline: score gap and the win probability it implies. No drawer needed for the common case.
  • The drawer stays for the full metric table, opened by choice rather than as the only way to see anything.
  • On a phone the dock is one line pinned to the bottom, expanding on tap.

How they combine

Smallest useful setC plus D. Both are pure layout, both give back vertical space above the fold, and neither touches the row design. lowest risk
Biggest readability gainA plus B. The board is the product; these two decide how it reads on each device. most visible
ConflictsD replaces the rail, so it supersedes the current rail panels including tier density. Everything else is independent. D vs rail only
Order if you take all fiveC, D, A, B, E. Chrome first, then layout, then the row, then mobile, then the new interaction. each ships alone
